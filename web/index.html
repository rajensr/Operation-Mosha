The 4-Stage Biological Progression (Why Rain Dictates Cases 3-4 Weeks Later)
Stage 1: Week t-4
Water Accumulation
Heavy rainfall creates stagnant pools in unsealed drums, construction pits, discarded tires, and blocked drains.
Stage 2: Week t-3
Larval Metamorphosis
Aedes eggs hatch into aquatic larvae and pupae. Development takes 7 to 10 days in warm ambient temperatures.
Stage 3: Week t-2
Vector Emergence & EIP
Adult mosquitoes feed. Virus replicates inside the vector during the 8 to 12 day Extrinsic Incubation Period.
Stage 4: Week t
Clinical Outbreak Surge
Infectious bites cause human infections. After 4 to 7 days intrinsic incubation, hospital admissions spike.

Key Epidemiological Insights for Public Health Administrators
The 3-4 Week Lag Primacy (+0.8066 vs. +0.5298)

Cumulative 4-week rainfall correlation (+0.8066) and lag-3 rainfall (+0.7499) drastically outweigh same-week precipitation (+0.5298). Waiting for rain to manifest as clinical cases before deploying larvicide guarantees an outbreak. Intervention must occur at weeks t-3 and t-2.

Thermal & Humidity Windows (+0.5256 & +0.3322)

Relative humidity exceeding 80% prevents adult mosquito desiccation, extending vector survival past the 8-12 day viral Extrinsic Incubation Period. Temperatures below 20 deg C or above 36 deg C halt transmission.

Statistical Formulation & Design Rationale

Because 58.03% of weekly ward records report zero dengue cases, single-stage regression suffers from zero-inflation collapse—predicting small fractions near zero and missing sharp outbreak spikes.

The Two-Stage Hurdle Model decomposes inference into two decoupled estimators:

Expected Cases = P(Cases > 0 | X) * E[Cases | Cases > 0, X]

Stage 1: HistGradientBoostingClassifier determines probability of outbreak occurrence.
Stage 2: HistGradientBoostingRegressor, trained strictly on positive case instances, estimates severity conditional on occurrence.
